**Official guidance:** Burn: cool under cool running water for 20 minutes (10+ acceptable); remove rings and tight items; cover loosely with cling film or a clean non-fluffy dressing; no ice, butter or creams; get care for large, deep or blistering burns, and burns to hands, face or genitals.

1. **Cool the burn**: Run cool tap water over the burn for 10-15 minutes, or apply a cool, wet compress. Never use ice or ice water, as this can cause further damage.

6. **Take pain relief**: Over-the-counter pain medications, such as acetaminophen or ibuprofen, can help alleviate pain and discomfort.

**When to Seek Medical Attention:**

* If the burn is severe (third-degree), covers a large area, or is on the face, hands, or feet.
* If you experience difficulty breathing or swallowing.
* If you have a fever or chills.
* If the burn is deep or has a white or charred appearance.
* If you have diabetes, heart disease, or a weakened immune system.
* If the burn is not improving or is showing signs of infection (redness, swelling, increased pain, or pus).

**Additional Tips:**

* Avoid breaking blisters, as this can lead to infection.
* Keep the burn area clean and dry.
* Avoid using antibiotics or other medications without consulting a doctor.
* Monitor the burn area for signs of infection and seek medical attention if you notice any unusual symptoms.

Remember, if you're unsure about the severity of the burn or need further guidance, it's always best to consult a healthcare professional for advice.
