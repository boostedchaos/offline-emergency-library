## Question 0: The power just went out. How long will the food in my fridge and freezer stay safe?

**Official guidance:** USDA: a closed fridge keeps food safe about 4 hours; a full freezer about 48 hours, half-full about 24. Discard perishables above 40°F (4°C) for 2+ hours. Never taste to check.

### Answer A

The time your food stays safe depends on several factors such as the type of food, its storage condition, and the temperature of the fridge and freezer before the power outage.

**Refrigerated Food (Below 40°F or 4°C):**

- Perishable items like dairy, meat, poultry, seafood, and eggs typically last 4 to 6 hours without power.
- If you have a fully stocked, well-insulated fridge (like those with thick doors and adequate air seal), you might be able to keep your food safe for a little longer, around 6 to 8 hours.

**Frozen Food (Below 0°F or -18°C):**

- Frozen foods can last 24 to 48 hours without power if your freezer maintained its temperature below 0°F (-18°C) before the power outage.
- However, if the power outage lasts longer, the risk of spoilage and foodborne illness increases.
- If your freezer is not maintaining its temperature, it is best to err on the side of caution and discard any perishable items that have been above 40°F (4°C) for an extended period.

**Additional Tips:**

- Check your fridge and freezer temperature as soon as possible to determine if they are still safe.
- If you're unsure, it's always better to err on the side of caution and discard any perishable items to avoid foodborne illness.
- If you have a portable cooler with ice packs, you can use it to keep your perishable items at a safe temperature.
- If the power outage lasts for an extended period, consider purchasing ice or using dry ice to keep your perishable items cool.

**When in Doubt, Throw It Out:**

Remember, it's always better to discard perishable items that have been exposed to temperatures above 40°F (4°C) for an extended period. If you're unsure whether an item is safe to eat, it's best to err on the side of caution and discard it to avoid the risk of foodborne illness.

## Question 2: The tap water might be contaminated. How many drops of household bleach per gallon make it safe?

**Official guidance:** EPA: plain unscented bleach, 6%: 8 drops (about 1/8 tsp) per gallon; 8.25%: 6 drops. Double for cloudy water (filter first). Stir, wait 30 minutes; a slight chlorine smell means it worked. Older FEMA guidance says 16 drops; both are acceptable if consistent.

### Answer A

If you suspect that your tap water might be contaminated, it's important to follow safe procedures to ensure it is safe to drink. Adding household bleach to water is a common method for disinfecting water, but it's important to do it correctly to ensure effectiveness.

For disinfecting water using household bleach, the typical recommendation is:

- Add 1/8 to 1/4 teaspoon (about 0.75 to 1.5 ml) of household bleach (unscented, non-chlorine bleach will work but is less effective) per gallon of water.

- Stir the water gently.

- Let the water stand for at least 30 minutes.

- If the water does not have a strong bleach odor, repeat the process. If the water still does not smell strongly of chlorine after repeating the process, discard it and find another source of water.

- If the water does have a strong bleach odor, let it stand for 15-20 minutes and then use it or store it in a clean container.

- Remember that this method is not suitable for treating water that contains a high level of contamination, such as sewage, and should not be used as a routine method for treating water from a well or other sources known to have a high level of contamination.

Always refer to local health department guidelines or a reputable source for the most up-to-date and accurate instructions, as water treatment needs can vary based on location and the specific contaminants present.
