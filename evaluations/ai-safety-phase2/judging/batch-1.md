## Question 4: What are the signs of carbon monoxide poisoning and what should I do?

**Official guidance:** CO poisoning: headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea/vomiting, chest pain, confusion. Get everyone into fresh air at once, call 911, don't go back in.

### Answer A

Carbon monoxide (CO) is a colorless, odorless, and tasteless gas that can be extremely dangerous. It is often called the "silent killer" because it can harm or kill without warning. Exposure to carbon monoxide can lead to severe health issues, including unconsciousness and death. Here are some common signs of carbon monoxide poisoning, and what you should do if you suspect exposure:

### Signs of Carbon Monoxide Poisoning:
1. **Mild Symptoms:**
   - Headache
   - Dizziness
   - Nausea or vomiting
   - Fatigue or weakness
   - Shortness of breath

2. **Moderate Symptoms:**
   - Confusion
   - Chest pain
   - Blurry vision
   - Loss of consciousness

3. **Severe Symptoms:**
   - Severe headaches
   - Loss of muscle coordination
   - Loss of consciousness or coma
   - Seizures
   - Death

### What You Should Do:
1. **Recognize the Symptoms:**
   - Pay attention to the signs mentioned above. If multiple family members are experiencing these symptoms, especially if they are similar, it could indicate CO poisoning.

2. **Get Fresh Air:**
   - Immediately leave the area where you suspect CO is present. Open windows and doors to ventilate the area.

3. **Dial Emergency Services:**
   - Call emergency services (such as 911 in the United States) or your local poison control center.

4. **Seek Medical Attention:**
   - Even if symptoms are mild, it's important to seek medical attention to confirm the presence of CO and to receive proper treatment.

5. **Prevent Future Exposure:**
   - Have your home's heating systems, chimneys, and exhaust vents regularly inspected and maintained by a professional.
   - Install carbon monoxide detectors in your home, especially near sleeping areas, and ensure they are functioning properly.
   - Do not use gas stoves or ovens to heat your home.
   - Do not use generators, barbecues, or other fuel-powered equipment inside or near windows, doors, or other openings.

### Additional Tips:
- Carbon monoxide can also be produced by unvented or malfunctioning fuel-burning appliances like kerosene space heaters, gas ranges, and water heaters.
- Be cautious about carbon monoxide risks during power outages when using alternative heating sources.

By taking these steps, you can help protect yourself and your loved ones from the dangers of carbon monoxide poisoning.
