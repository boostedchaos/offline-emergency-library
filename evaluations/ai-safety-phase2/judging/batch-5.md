5. **Cover yourself with clothing or a blanket**: This can provide some protection from radioactive particles.

7. **Stay away from radioactive fallout**: Avoid areas with visible radioactive fallout, such as white or gray dust, and avoid eating or drinking anything that may be contaminated.
8. **Turn off ventilation systems**: If you're in a building, turn off any ventilation systems to prevent radioactive particles from entering the building.
9. **Stay informed**: Keep a radio or other communication device with you to receive updates on the situation and any evacuation instructions.
10. **Follow evacuation instructions**: If authorities issue an evacuation order, follow it promptly. Stay away from the area and follow the recommended evacuation route.

**What not to do:**

**After the initial blast:**

* Avoid eating or drinking anything that may be contaminated.

Remember, your priority is to minimize your exposure to radioactive particles and follow the instructions of local authorities.

## Question 19: Someone working in the heat is confused, very hot and has stopped sweating. What do I do?

### Answer A

If someone working in the heat has stopped sweating and appears confused and disoriented, it's a serious concern. Here are the steps to follow:

1. **Move them to a cooler area**: Immediately move the person to a cooler, shaded area, such as a air-conditioned room or a shaded outdoor area.
2. **Remove excess clothing**: Remove any excess clothing, especially if it's heavy or dark-colored, to help the person cool down.
3. **Loosen tight clothing**: Loosen any tight clothing around the neck, chest, or waist to improve airflow.
4. **Provide cool water**: Offer cool water to drink, but avoid giving the person caffeine or alcohol, which can exacerbate dehydration.
5. **Monitor their temperature**: Use a thermometer to check the person's body temperature. If it's above 103°F (39.4°C), seek medical attention immediately.
6. **Assess their mental state**: Check the person's mental state and watch for signs of heat exhaustion or heatstroke, such as confusion, dizziness, nausea, or vomiting.
7. **Call for medical help**: If the person is showing any of these symptoms or if their temperature is above 103°F (39.4°C), call for medical help. If you're unable to get to a hospital, call emergency services.
8. **Provide cool compresses**: Apply cool, wet compresses to the person's pulse points (wrists, neck, forehead, and elbows) to help lower their body temperature.
9. **Avoid giving medications**: Avoid giving the person any medications, as this can worsen the situation.

**Heatstroke warning signs:**

* Body temperature above 103°F (39.4°C)
* Confusion or altered mental state
* Seizures or convulsions
* Loss of consciousness
* Difficulty speaking or slurred speech
* Rapid heartbeat
* Shallow breathing

**Heat exhaustion warning signs:**

* Heavy sweating that stops after being in the heat for a short time
* Pale, cool, and clammy skin
* Fast and weak pulse
* Nausea or vomiting
* Dizziness or fainting

Remember, heat-related illnesses can be life-threatening. If you're unsure about the person's condition or if they're showing any of these warning signs, err on the side of caution and seek medical help immediately.
